The Quick Answer

Paint protection film (PPF) is a thick, clear urethane film that physically absorbs rock chips and scratches. Ceramic coating is a thin, hard chemical layer that adds gloss, repels water and makes washing easier, but it won't stop a rock chip. If you drive Hwy 97 or the Okanagan Connector (Hwy 97C) often, PPF on the front end plus ceramic coating on the rest of the car gives you the best of both.

We install both every week at our Kelowna studio, so this guide is based on what we actually see on customer vehicles, not marketing claims.

What Is Paint Protection Film (PPF)?

PPF is a transparent, flexible film applied over your painted panels. Premium films have a self-healing top layer: light scratches and swirls fade away when exposed to heat or sun. XPEL notes that deeper scratches that cut through the film may not heal and might need the panel re-filmed.

Top-tier films such as XPEL Ultimate Plus carry a 10-year manufacturer warranty. Learn more about our paint protection film service.

What Is Ceramic Coating?

Ceramic coating is a liquid that bonds to your clear coat and cures into a hard, glass-like layer. It makes paint glossier, sheds water, resists bird droppings, sap and UV, and makes dirt release easily when you wash.

What it can't do is absorb impact. A coating is measured in microns; a rock at highway speed goes straight through it. See our ceramic coating packages, or read more on the benefits of ceramic coating.

Which One Should You Choose?

Choose PPF if…

  • You commute on Hwy 97 or drive the Okanagan Connector to Vancouver regularly (check DriveBC for current road conditions).
  • You own a new, leased, or high-value vehicle and want to avoid repainting.
  • You plan to keep the car for many years or sell it in top condition.

Choose ceramic coating if…

  • You mostly drive around town in Kelowna, West Kelowna or Lake Country.
  • Your priority is gloss, easy washing, and protection from sun, sap and bird droppings.
  • You want whole-vehicle protection at a lower price than full PPF.

Choose both if…

  • You want the front end protected from chips and the whole car easy to clean.
  • You want maximum resale value. Coating over PPF also keeps the film cleaner and more hydrophobic.

The most popular combo we install is front-end PPF (bumper, hood, fenders, mirrors) with a ceramic coating over the entire car.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PPF better than ceramic coating?

Neither is better overall; they do different jobs. PPF protects against physical damage like rock chips. Ceramic coating protects against chemical and UV damage and makes washing easier.

Can you put ceramic coating on top of PPF?

Yes, and we recommend it. Coating the film adds gloss, makes it hydrophobic, and helps prevent staining from bugs and road grime.

Does ceramic coating prevent rock chips?

No. A coating is far too thin to absorb the impact of a stone. Only PPF provides real chip protection.

How long does PPF last?

Premium films last up to 10 years with proper care and carry a 10-year manufacturer warranty. Budget films may yellow or peel much sooner.

Is PPF worth it in Kelowna?

For most drivers who use Hwy 97 regularly, yes, at least on the front end. Repainting a bumper and hood usually costs more than filming them.

Key Takeaways

  • PPF stops rock chips and self-heals light scratches; ceramic coating does not stop chips.
  • Ceramic coating gives the best gloss, water beading and easy washing.
  • For Okanagan highway driving, front-end PPF plus a full ceramic coating is the strongest setup.
  • Paint should be corrected before either product goes on.
